Real vs Simulated

Real controls. Synthetic data.

Simulated

Afterglow Labs (fictional AV touring studio), September 2026 scenario, Maya's Steam purchase, EUR crew deposit, Chase/PayPal/Ramp transaction data.

Real Code

Single ledger gateway (only write path), role-based authorization (HTTP 403), state machine (new → proposed → needs_info → approved → posted), SHA-256 hash-chained audit log, challenge/defend/concede with precedent recording.

Why the synthetic data is realistic

Afterglow Labs is synthetic so no real client's books are exposed, but it's modeled on how real books look:

  • QuickBooks-style chart of accounts: 1010 Cash, 1250 Employee Receivable, 1310 Prepaid, 6410 Software, 6810 Bank & FX Fees
  • Real bank-descriptor formats: "PAYPAL *INST XFER", "ORIG CO NAME:STRIPE"
  • Multi-source feeds: Bank statements, payment processors (PayPal/Stripe), corporate cards (Ramp), GL exports
  • To-the-cent amounts including FX fees: $3,912.44 total with $147.50 wire fees
  • Materiality thresholds & written policies: P-0317 (personal purchases → Employee Receivable)
  • Real close problems: Bundled payments, personal spend on corporate cards, timing & accruals, duplicate vendors, vendor bank-change fraud

What's real: The reasoning, the AI calls, the controls (403 denial), and the hash-chained audit log. This is not derived from any real company.
